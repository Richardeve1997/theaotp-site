01How it fits

Four parts, one place for the work.

Space holds what your team knows. Your dot does the ongoing work. You approve it. A Site shows the result to everyone who needs it.

In the Reel I called this ChatGPT 2.0. That's my name for it. OpenAI calls it ChatGPT Work, and the names below are OpenAI's.

You and your teamThe shared SpaceChatGPT Work

What each part does

PartWhat it does
SpaceA shared home for your team's pages, slides, spreadsheets and files. It replaced Library. ChatGPT and your dot can use what's in it as context, along with tools you connect, such as Google Drive and Slack.
PagesThe multiplayer partA page is a document you and ChatGPT edit together. Everyone with edit access can work on it at the same time, each with their own ChatGPT, and you can tag @ChatGPT or your dot in the text or in a comment to make a change. Sharing a page doesn't share your private chats.
SitesWebsites and tools you build by describing them: dashboards, team trackers, client portals, calculators. You choose who can visit, from selected people to your workspace or the public, and teammates in your workspace can edit.
DotsAn always-on agent with its own computer in the cloud. You hand it a responsibility, and it keeps the work moving between conversations and brings you anything that needs a decision. You reach it in ChatGPT, Slack or Microsoft Teams, and you decide what it can do alone and what needs your approval.

Who can use what

PartPlans
SpacePro, Business and Enterprise. You create and edit on web and desktop. On mobile you can read and share pages for now.
SitesPublic beta on eligible Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu plans. Hosting is free until 31 December 2026. From 1 January 2027 you pay for usage above your plan's allowance.
DotsRolling out on Pro, Business Premium and Enterprise, where an admin has to switch them on. On Pro you need to be over 18 and outside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland.

03Before you start

Set up the shared context once.

The same five steps sit under every team setup. Do them once, and each setup below builds on them.

  • 1Check your plan.Space needs Pro, Business or Enterprise, and dots are still rolling out. The table in section 01 has the details.
  • 2Connect the tools the work needs.Google Drive, Slack, your CRM, your issue tracker. Each one is connected and given permission on its own: adding your dot to Slack doesn't give it your other apps.
  • 3Make a team Space.Put plans, definitions, past decisions and source links into pages, and keep the working document on one main page. Give that page instructions: what to update, which sources to use and when to check.
  • 4Give your dot one job.Name the sources, the page it keeps current and what should make it message you. Add it to the Slack channel and tell it to watch, because adding it alone doesn't start monitoring. Then set a Custom Rule so it asks you before it shares or publishes anything.
  • 5Review the first run together.Each teammate can ask their own ChatGPT or dot to check or revise the same page. Build a Site once people need a dashboard or a tracker. For a report, a shared page is often enough.
Schedules. If you want something done at a set time, give a time zone and an end date, and ask your dot to confirm the schedule it saved. Writing "every Monday" on a page doesn't schedule anything by itself.
No dot yet? Space gets you most of the way. Ask ChatGPT in the page's chat to update the page, and set up an automation for the recurring updates. Add the dot when it reaches your account.

The five setups that follow are examples I'd start with, not templates OpenAI ships. Each one builds on the uses OpenAI lists for that team, and I link that page under each setup so you can see what's documented and what's my suggestion.

04Finance

Finance: a month-end pack that stays current.

Example setup

Your dot explains the month's variances and drafts the forecast changes. Your finance lead approves before anyone else sees a number.

You approveThe shared SpaceChatGPT Work
PartFinance setup
In the SpaceThe budget, the actuals workbook, approved forecast assumptions, close notes, and a page of definitions and past decisions.
The dot watchesNew close notes as they land, and your finance channel in Slack.
The dot doesExplains the biggest variances with the source behind each figure, keeps actuals apart from assumptions, and drafts forecast changes on the shared page.
You approveYour finance lead checks the calculations and assumptions. Nothing reaches leadership before that.
The Site showsA leadership summary with the approved numbers, what's driving them and the risks.
Your teamDepartment heads add context on the same page with their own ChatGPT.

Starter prompt for your dot

Use the budget, actuals and close notes linked in this Space to prepare our monthly performance pack. Explain the largest variances and show the source behind each figure. Keep actuals separate from forecast assumptions. Draft proposed forecast changes on the shared page for finance review, and don't share any figures outside this Space until I approve them. Keep the page current as new close notes arrive, and message me in Slack when a change needs a decision.

Then build the Site

Build a Site called Monthly performance for our leadership team. Show the approved figures from this month's performance pack: revenue and costs against budget, the three biggest variances with one line on each, and the risks to the forecast. Share it with the leadership team only.
What OpenAI documents

OpenAI's finance page lists budget variance, cash forecasting and revenue dashboards among its finance prompts, and says sensitive actions stay subject to human review. Its dots page describes a finance lead whose dot tracks revenue, refreshes the investor deck and shares the latest numbers with the team once they give the go-ahead.

05Operations

Operations: the weekly review, ready by Monday.

Example setup

Your dot reads the trackers and team updates, flags what's stuck and who owns it, and has the review ready before Monday's meeting.

You approveThe shared SpaceChatGPT Work
PartOperations setup
In the SpaceProject trackers, KPI definitions, this quarter's priorities and an agreed list of owners.
The dot watchesTeam updates in your ops channel and changes to the trackers.
The dot doesLists progress, blockers, the owner and the next decision for every project, and flags conflicting updates and missing owners.
You approveYour ops lead signs off the decisions list before the meeting. The dot only messages them when a milestone is at risk.
The Site showsA status board for leaders: what's on track, what's stuck and where to act.
Your teamTeam leads add context on the same page before the review.

Starter prompt for your dot

Keep our weekly operating review current using the trackers and team updates linked in this Space. For each project, list progress, blockers, the owner and the next decision. Flag conflicting updates and missing owners. Make routine changes on the page, and message me in Slack only when a milestone is at risk or a decision is needed. Have the review ready by 9am Monday, [your time zone], for the next four weeks, and confirm the schedule you save.

Then build the Site

Build a Site called Operating review for our team leads. Show every project from the review page with its status, owner and next decision, and put anything at risk at the top. Share it with our workspace.
What OpenAI documents

OpenAI's operations page describes pulling project updates from across your systems to keep weekly reviews current, and building dashboards with Sites that show leaders where to act. The dots docs include a scheduled check-in: the dot checks a channel on weekday mornings for four weeks and only messages you about deadlines at risk or decisions.

06Marketing

Marketing: a launch that keeps up with the product.

Example setup

Your dot follows the launch channel, spots product changes that affect the campaign and drafts the fixes. The campaign owner decides what goes out.

PartMarketing setup
In the SpaceThe product roadmap, customer research, the launch brief, brand guidance, approved visuals, and the shared brief and deck.
The dot watchesThe launch channel in Slack.
The dot doesProposes three campaign directions to start. Once you pick one, it flags product changes that affect the campaign and prepares revised copy, slides and speaker notes.

Using our product roadmap, customer research, launch brief and brand guidance, propose three campaign directions, with the audience insight and tradeoffs behind each. Once we pick one, keep the shared brief and draft slides in step with the launch channel. When a product detail changes, prepare revised copy, slides and speaker notes for my review. Bring decisions to me in Slack, and leave publishing to the campaign owner.

Then build the Site

Build a Site called Launch hub for the marketing, product and sales teams. Show the launch calendar, the approved messaging and the approved assets from this Space, newest first. Share it with our workspace.

08Engineering

Engineering: feedback in, reviewed fix out.

Example setup

Your dot watches incoming feedback, reproduces the problem and has Codex draft the smallest fix. An engineer reviews it before anything merges.

You approveThe shared SpaceChatGPT Work
PartEngineering setup
In the SpaceReproduction steps, technical constraints, your definition of done and an investigation page.
The dot watchesYour feedback source, such as a Slack channel or issue tracker, and the code project.
The dot doesReproduces the problem, finds the cause, asks Codex for the smallest fix with checks, and writes the findings and validation on the page.
You approveAn engineer reviews the change. Merging and deploying stay with them.
The Site showsA fix tracker for the rest of the company: reported, being fixed, in review, shipped.
Your teamProduct weighs in on the page when a fix needs a decision.

Starter prompt for your dot

Watch the feedback channel and the code project linked here. When someone reports a problem, reproduce it, find the cause and ask Codex to prepare the smallest fix with the right checks. Write the findings, the affected behaviour and how you validated the fix on this page. Prepare the change for code review and message me in Slack when it's ready or a product decision is needed. Merging and deploying stay with the engineering owner.

Then build the Site

Build a Site called Fix tracker for the whole company. List every reported problem from the investigation page with its status: reported, being fixed, in review or shipped. Share it with our workspace.
What OpenAI documents

The dots docs describe this one: connect the feedback source and the code project, and the dot investigates reported problems and prepares a fix for code review before merging. OpenAI's engineering page uses the prompt "Investigate this issue and prepare the smallest tested fix for review".

09What to do now

Pick one team. Run it for a week.

Start with the job your team chases every week, and give your dot just that one.

  • If you run the numbers: start at 04, Finance.
  • If you run the weekly meeting: start at 05, Operations.
  • If you're launching something: start at 06, Marketing.
  • If you're working a big deal: start at 07, Sales.
  • If bug reports and requests keep piling up: start at 08, Engineering.
  • If you don't have Space yet: check your plan in section 01, then start with one shared page and ChatGPT.

After a week, look at what it got wrong and tighten the instructions on the page. That's most of the work.
