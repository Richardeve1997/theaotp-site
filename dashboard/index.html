01How it works

Five steps, all in one Finance project.

You upload your bank statements once. ChatGPT turns them into a budget report, finds costs you could cut, builds you a private dashboard and helps you update it every month.

YouChatGPTYour private dashboard

Every step has a prompt you can copy. The report and the cost check work in any ChatGPT project. The dashboard needs ChatGPT Work with Sites, and the monthly update uses a scheduled task.

What you need

StepWhat it needs
Report and cost checkA ChatGPT project. Projects come with Free, Go, Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu. A project holds up to 5 files on Free, 25 on Go and Plus, and 40 on Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu. You can upload 10 at a time.
The dashboardChatGPT Work and Sites. Sites is in public beta on eligible Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu plans. What you get depends on your plan, region and workspace settings.

02Privacy

Know what you're sharing before you upload.

Bank statements say a lot about you. Take out what ChatGPT doesn't need, and check one setting before you start.

  • 1Remove what identifies you.Black out or delete account and card numbers, your address and any reference numbers. Keep the dates, amounts and merchant names. That's all the report needs.
  • 2Decide on model training.On personal Free, Plus and Pro accounts, Improve the model for everyone is on by default. While it's on, OpenAI says it may use information from your projects to train its models. To turn it off, open Settings, select Data controls and switch it off. Your chats stay where they are.
  • 3Skip the thumbs.If you rate a response with a thumbs up or down, OpenAI says the whole conversation may be used for training, even with that setting off.
  • 4Know how to delete it.You can delete any file from the project's sources list. Deleting the whole project removes its chats, instructions and files, and OpenAI says it clears them from its systems within 30 days unless it has to keep them for legal or security reasons.
On Business, Enterprise and Edu workspaces, OpenAI doesn't train on your content by default, and your organisation's own retention rules apply. The dashboard starts private too: a new Site opens only for you, plus workspace admins on a work account, until you change who can visit.
03Set up

Make a Finance project and add your statements.

A project keeps your statements, chats and instructions in one place, so every prompt on this page works from the same files.

  • 1Create the project.Select New project in the sidebar and call it Finance. It works in your browser and in the desktop app.
  • 2Download your statements.Get the last few months from each bank account and credit card, as PDFs or spreadsheet exports. Three months is a good start, and more history gives the estimates more to work with.
  • 3Upload them to the project.Add them to the project's sources, up to 10 files at a time, so every chat in the project can use them. ChatGPT doesn't connect to your bank here. It only sees what you upload.
Keep the project on default memory. OpenAI's help page says ChatGPT Work isn't available in a project that uses project-only memory, and you need Work for the dashboard. Shared projects always use project-only memory, which is one more reason to keep this one to yourself.
In the US, OpenAI also has Finances, which links bank accounts to ChatGPT through Plaid. You don't need it for anything on this page.
04The report

Find out where your money actually goes.

Start a new chat in the Finance project and paste prompt 1. You get a clean table of every transaction and a report for each month: income, spending by category, bills, subscriptions and what's left.

Prompt 1 · The budget report

Use the bank statements and transaction exports uploaded to this project to prepare a personal budget report. First, tell me which accounts and dates the files cover. If something that affects the answer is missing, ask me rather than making it up.

Create a transaction table with account, date, merchant or description, amount, currency and category. Flag uncertain categories and possible duplicates. Match transfers between my own accounts so they don't inflate my income or spending. Match credit card repayments to the card purchases where you can, so nothing is counted twice. List any transactions you can't match for me to review, rather than leaving them out without telling me.

For each complete month, summarise my income, spending by category, regular bills, subscriptions and the money left after spending. Keep transfers to savings separate from income and spending. Point out unusual changes, and only compare equivalent periods. Check the totals against the statements and explain any difference.

Finish with the three things most worth reviewing first.
Check the totals against your statements before you go further, because everything after this builds on them. If it counts a transfer between your own accounts as spending, say so and ask it to fix the table.
05Cut costs

Then ask where you could spend less.

Stay in the same chat and paste prompt 2. It works from your real spending, and for things like phone plans and insurance it's told to check current prices before it suggests a switch.

Prompt 2 · The cost check

Using the budget report and transaction table from this chat, show me where I could cut costs, based on my real spending. Look at subscriptions, regular bills that have gone up, and the categories where most of my money goes.

I live in [your country and state]. For phone plans, insurance and other bills where switching could save money, first ask me what cover, data or features I need. Then search the web for current offers on the providers' own websites. For each one, show the price, the main conditions, any fees and the link. Don't invent deals, and don't call any saving guaranteed.

For every suggestion, show the estimated saving per month and per year, the transactions it's based on and what I'd give up. Put the easiest ones first.
Fill in where you live, because offers depend on it. Then open every link and check the offer yourself before you switch. A cheaper policy can also cover less, which is why the prompt asks what cover you need first.
06Dashboard

Build a private dashboard in ChatGPT Work.

Start a ChatGPT Work chat in the same project and paste prompt 3. It asks what you expect to earn and spend, then uses Sites to build a dashboard of your spending, your savings goal and estimates for the rest of the year.

A sketch of the layout prompt 3 asks for. Yours will look different.

Prompt 3 · The dashboard

In ChatGPT Work, use Sites to build a private, interactive dashboard from the transaction table and budget report in this project. If it helps, create a clean spreadsheet or CSV from the report first. Keep the Site private and show it to me for review.

Before you build it, ask me for my expected income, upcoming bills, irregular costs (such as car registration or holidays), my savings target and the date I want to reach it. Treat anything I don't know as unknown, and tell me what you can't estimate yet. My bank accounts are not connected, so don't treat any figure as live.

Show my monthly income and spending, spending by category, regular bills and subscriptions, and progress towards my savings target. Keep actual figures clearly separate from estimates of spending and savings for the end of this month and the end of the year. Base the estimates on my history, known bills and the assumptions I give you. For each estimate, explain the calculation, which months of data it uses, what's missing and when the data was last updated. Check that the historical totals match the budget report.

Add date and category filters, and a scenario panel where I can try changes such as spending less on takeaway or cancelling a subscription. Show the difference per month and by my target date. Keep one-off savings separate from monthly ones, and never present a future saving as guaranteed.

Add a short list of practical suggestions, each linked to the transactions behind it, with the estimated effect and the assumption it relies on. Don't count transfers between my own accounts as spending or income. Show uncertain categories and any totals that don't reconcile, so I can correct them.

When I add new statements, update the transaction table, report, charts and estimates, and explain the main changes. Don't set up a schedule yet.

Then try asking

  • 01If I spent half as much on takeaway, how much more would I have by the end of the year?
  • 02Which subscriptions could I cancel, and what would that add up to over a year?
  • 03Am I on track for my savings target? If not, what would get me there?
  • 04Which categories have crept up over the last three months?
Estimates are only as good as the history and assumptions behind them, so read the assumptions the dashboard shows. Building and editing a Site counts towards your plan's usage. Hosting is free until 31 December 2026. From 1 January 2027 you pay only for usage above your plan's monthly allowance.
07Every month

Keep it current with a monthly task.

A scheduled task reminds you on the 1st of every month to add your new statements. The dashboard then picks up the new month, explains what changed and updates its suggestions.

OpenAI's help page says a task created inside a project can't read that project's files, so there are two ways to run the update.

Hands off. Save each month's statements to a Google Drive folder, connect Google Drive in ChatGPT Work, and paste the folder link into prompt 4. Each run reads the folder and updates the dashboard itself. OpenAI's own example of a dashboard that stays up to date works the same way.

By hand. Delete the folder line from prompt 4, and the task becomes your monthly reminder. When it arrives, upload the statements to your Finance project and paste prompt 5 into a ChatGPT Work chat there.

Prompt 4 · The monthly task

Paste it into a ChatGPT Work chat in your browser. A task started in the browser keeps running when your laptop is off.

Create a standalone scheduled task called Monthly budget refresh. Run it on the 1st of every month at 9am, my time.

On each run:
1. Remind me to add last month's bank statements.
2. Check this Google Drive folder for new statements: [paste the folder link, or delete this line to upload by hand]
3. If there are new statements, update my private dashboard Site, [your Site's name]: the transactions, the budget report, the charts and the estimates. Compare the new month with earlier months, explain the main changes and suggest what to change next.
4. Review the assumptions behind the estimates and my savings target. Tell me which ones changed and why.
5. Check the totals against the statements and list anything that doesn't reconcile.

If there are no new statements yet, send the reminder only and change nothing. Keep the dashboard private. Don't share it, publish it or change who can see it.

Prompt 5 · The update, by hand

I've added last month's statements to this project. Update the transaction table, the budget report and my dashboard Site with them. Compare the new month with earlier months, explain the main changes and suggest what to change next. Tell me which assumptions behind the estimates changed and why, and list anything that doesn't reconcile with the statements. Keep the dashboard private.
Keep account numbers and other financial details out of the task's title and instructions. OpenAI warns that anyone with a shared link to a task can read them. Check the first run's figures against your statements before you rely on it.
08What to do now

Start with the report. Add the rest when it's useful.

You don't need all five steps on day one. The report on its own shows you where your money goes.

  • If you just want to see where it goes: do sections 03 and 04. Any plan with Projects can.
  • If money's tight this month: run prompt 2 straight after the report and start with the easiest three suggestions.
  • If you're saving for something: build the dashboard and give it your target and date.
  • If you'll check it every month: set up prompt 4, then check the first run against your statements.
  • If you need advice on debt, investing or tax: take the report to a licensed adviser. It gives them a clear picture to start from.

After the first month, tell it what it got wrong, such as a payment in the wrong category, so the next update starts from better data.
