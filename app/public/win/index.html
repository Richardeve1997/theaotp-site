01 · The starting point

A useful library. Your business context.

The skills give your agent a process. Your context gives it something useful to work with.

Corey Haines built Marketing Skills. The library covers jobs such as copywriting, SEO, conversion and growth. Its product-marketing skill supplies the shared context the other skills check before they work.

The prompt below sets that up, then interviews you for the three things I covered in the video: product strategy, your brand and copy bible, and a design system.

I did not create Corey's library and earn nothing from linking to it. This setup prompt applies the workflow from my Reel.
02 · The setup prompt

Paste this. Answer the questions.

Open your business project in Claude Code or Codex, then paste the prompt.

Have your website, offer, a few pieces of approved copy and any brand files nearby. You can supply what you have during the interview. The agent should identify gaps instead of making things up.

Help me set up a marketing system for my business using Corey Haines's Marketing Skills library:
https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills

Work in my current project. Start by checking the project's instructions, existing skills and marketing documents. Preserve useful material and do not overwrite existing files without showing me the proposed changes.

1. Set up the library
Read the repository's current README and installation instructions. Use the documented method that fits my agent and workspace, whether Claude Code or Codex. Confirm that the skills are available. If you cannot install them in this environment, explain the specific missing capability and give me the shortest supported setup step. Do not claim installation succeeded unless you have checked it.

Read the product-marketing skill before the other marketing skills. Use its current context-file location and format rather than inventing an incompatible location. Identify which of its related skills cover content, SEO, paid marketing, sales and go-to-market.

2. Interview me for about 10 minutes
First inspect any business information I have already supplied. Then ask focused questions, one at a time, and skip anything the documents already answer. Keep the interview to around 10 questions. Ask a follow-up only when the missing detail would change the work.

Cover:
- What do we sell, to whom, at what price, and what action should a prospect take?
- What urgent problem are buyers trying to solve? What have they tried already?
- What makes our offer different, and what evidence supports that difference?
- What objections, buying triggers and exact customer phrases do we hear?
- What channels currently bring customers? What have we tried and learned?
- What do our best existing copy and creative examples have in common?
- What should our brand sound like, and what wording or claims should it avoid?
- What visual rules, fonts, colours, logos and reusable components already exist?
- What is our next business goal, and what time, budget and team capacity do we have?
- What first marketing job would be most useful to complete this week?

Let me paste links, notes and examples instead of retyping them. Separate verified facts from assumptions. Never invent customer quotes, results, testimonials or evidence. If I do not know an answer, mark the gap and keep going.

3. Turn the answers into three working context documents
Create or update these in a clear marketing-context folder, reusing existing documents where possible:

PRODUCT STRATEGY
Audience and buyer roles; the problem and buying trigger; offer and pricing; positioning and differentiation; evidence; objections; goals; channels; practical constraints. Include source links or document references for factual claims.

BRAND AND COPY BIBLE
Voice rules; approved vocabulary; phrases to avoid; value propositions; customer language; objection handling; proof points; calls to action; a few approved examples and why they work. Label newly drafted examples as drafts until I approve them.

DESIGN SYSTEM
Existing colours, typography, logos, imagery, layout rules and reusable components. Reference the real source files. If no design system exists, create a small proposed starting version and clearly separate it from approved rules. Do not pass guesses off as an established brand.

Keep the documents concise enough to use during real work. Put unanswered questions and assumptions in a short, visible section.

4. Connect the context to the skills
Update the product-marketing context using the library's actual instructions. Reference the three documents so the other marketing skills can use the same audience, positioning, voice, proof and design decisions.

Add a short project-level instruction in the correct existing agent guidance file: before marketing work, read product-marketing context and the relevant brand documents, then use the skill that fits the job. Preserve existing project instructions. Do not edit every upstream library skill to duplicate the same business facts.

Show me which files you created or updated and how context gets loaded. Explain what is ready and what still needs a decision. Installing skills alone does not connect analytics, email or ad accounts; list any needed integrations separately.

5. Complete one useful first job
Based on my goal, choose one small deliverable and explain the choice. For example: use customer research and copywriting to draft a landing-page section, use the conversion skill to review it, then propose one measurable test.

Use our actual context. Show the source facts behind the draft, flag unsupported claims, and check it against our voice and design rules. Keep it as a draft for my review. Do not publish, send messages, spend money or change live campaigns without my explicit instruction.

Finish with the deliverable, the context-file locations, the next two useful actions, and a short explanation of how to save future corrections so the system improves. Begin now by inspecting the project and asking the first unanswered question.
03 · What you get

Three documents. One shared reference.

Check these before asking the system to produce a week's worth of work.

  • Product strategyWho buys, why they buy, what you offer, what proves it, and what you are trying to achieve.
  • Brand and copy bibleYour voice, customer language, approved claims, useful examples and calls to action.
  • Design systemThe visual rules and assets your business already uses, with proposals clearly marked when something is missing.

The product-marketing context should point to these documents. Other skills can then work from the same decisions instead of asking you to explain the business again.

04 · Use it today

Start with one job. Save what you learn.

A small piece of real work will show you what the context is missing.

  • Starting from scratch? Complete the interview and review the three documents first.
  • Already have good brand documents? Give the agent those files and ask it to fill the gaps.
  • Ready to use it? Draft one landing-page section or campaign asset, review it, then save useful corrections in the shared context.

The prompt produces a working setup and a first draft. Publishing, account connections and paid campaigns need their own decisions.
