Start here

Twenty, in one minute.

Twenty is a free, open-source CRM. It covers the core jobs most small businesses pay Salesforce for: contacts, companies, a deal pipeline, workflows and AI agents.

Here's the link: github.com/twentyhq/twenty. The repo describes itself as "The open alternative to Salesforce, designed for AI." and had 58,522 stars on GitHub on 19 October 2026. The website is twenty.com.

01What it replaces

What Twenty replaces.

Twenty keeps your contacts and companies, tracks deals through a pipeline, automates the repetitive steps and lets AI work on your data.

If you're coming from Salesforce, this is how the main records line up, from Twenty's migration guide.

In SalesforceIn Twenty
AccountCompany
ContactPeople
OpportunityOpportunity, shown as a kanban pipeline
ActivityTask or Note
Custom objectCustom object (unlimited on every plan)

Around those records you get table, kanban and calendar views, a visual workflow builder, an AI chat that answers questions about your data, AI agents inside workflows, email and calendar sync with Google or Microsoft, and REST and GraphQL APIs for your own tools.

Where Salesforce still wins, in Twenty's own words: "Salesforce remains the deepest enterprise platform, with an ecosystem for almost everything. At a thousand seats with a dedicated admin team, it is a different conversation." For a small business, the question is whether you use that depth.

The 3 September price change

On 3 September 2026 Salesforce replaced its Enterprise, Unlimited and Agentforce 1 editions with Core, Advanced and Max. Core is $195 a user a month. The Enterprise edition it replaced listed at $175, so that's about 11% more. Advanced at $395 replaced Unlimited at $350, about 13% more. Max stayed at $550. The old prices come from UpperEdge's analysis, not from Salesforce.

Salesforce frames it as more value, not a price rise. Its announcement says existing customers on legacy editions keep their pricing, and each new edition includes a pool of Flex Credits for AI (500,000 a year on Core). AI use beyond the pool is billed, and Agentforce for Sales is sold separately from $125 a user a month. So some AI is bundled now, and the rest costs extra.

Self-hosting costs nothing in licence fees, but it does take work. You need a server with at least 2 GB of RAM, HTTPS, backups and someone to run updates. Single sign-on, row-level permissions, AI usage data and audit logs need the paid Organization plan even when you self-host. Most of Twenty's code is under the AGPLv3 licence. Using it doesn't affect you, but if you change the code, read the licence first.
03Setup

Cloud or self-host? Pick one.

Pick the cloud if you want it working today with no server to look after. Self-host if you want it free and someone on your team is happy running Docker.

  • 1The cloud: sign up and go.

    Go to app.twenty.com and sign up with Google, Microsoft or email. Twenty's quickstart offers a 30-day trial with a card or 7 days without one, both with full access. Then name your workspace. You can change plan or billing period any time in Settings → Billing.

  • 2Self-host with Docker: one command.

    You need Docker and Docker Compose, installed and up to date, and at least 2 GB of RAM. Docker is a program that runs software in a sealed box, and it works on a Mac, a Windows PC or a Linux server. This is Twenty's one-line installer. Run it in a terminal, the text window where you type commands.

    bash <(curl -sL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/twentyhq/twenty/main/packages/twenty-docker/scripts/install.sh)

    When it finishes, open http://localhost:3000 in your browser. Trying it on your own laptop first is a good way to see it before you pay for a server.

  • 3Or do it by hand.

    The manual route from the same docs. Download the settings file, make two secrets, put them in .env, then start it.

    curl -o .env https://raw.githubusercontent.com/twentyhq/twenty/refs/heads/main/packages/twenty-docker/.env.example
openssl rand -base64 32
openssl rand -hex 32
curl -o docker-compose.yml https://raw.githubusercontent.com/twentyhq/twenty/refs/heads/main/packages/twenty-docker/docker-compose.yml
docker compose up -d

    Before the last line, open .env. Paste the first secret in as ENCRYPTION_KEY and the second as PG_DATABASE_PASSWORD. The docs say to keep the password free of special characters, which is why it uses the hex one. Keep ENCRYPTION_KEY somewhere safe: lose it and you lose access to every secret stored in the database, such as connected email accounts.

  • 4Put it on a server your team can reach.

    Set SERVER_URL in .env to the address people will use, such as https://crm.yourcompany.com, then restart with docker compose down and docker compose up -d. Twenty recommends a reverse proxy with SSL in front of it, because some features need HTTPS.

    Back up the database every day. This line from the docs goes in your crontab, the scheduler on Mac and Linux:

    0 2 * * * docker exec twenty-postgres pg_dump -U postgres twenty > /backups/twenty_$(date +\%Y\%m\%d).sql

    To upgrade: back up, run docker compose down, change TAG in .env to the new version, then docker compose up -d. Twenty runs the database changes itself when it starts.

Commands from Twenty's Docker Compose guide and upgrade guide, checked on 19 October 2026. If you'd rather not run a server, Twenty lists certified hosting partners on its site.
04First 10 minutes

Your first 10 minutes.

Connect your mailbox, then build your deal pipeline. That's enough to start using it tomorrow morning.

  • 1Connect your mailbox first.

    Go to Settings → Accounts and connect your Google or Microsoft account. Twenty imports your emails and calendar events and creates contacts from the people you talk to. Using another provider? The same page takes SMTP for mail and CalDAV for calendars. Twenty's own docs say to start here, because you get real data before you customise anything.

  • 2Set your deal stages.

    Your pipeline's columns come from the Stage field on Opportunities. Go to Settings → Data Model → Opportunities → Stage and rename the stages to match how you sell. The docs suggest five to seven, for example New, Qualified, Meeting, Proposal, Negotiation, Closed Won and Closed Lost.

  • 3Make the pipeline view.

    Open Opportunities, click the view dropdown, then + Add view. Call it "Sales Pipeline", click Create, then choose Kanban under Options. Under Options → Fields, show Company, Amount, Close Date and Owner. Click the number next to a stage name and pick Sum of Amount to see the value of each column.

  • 4Add a deal and drag it.

    Click + in a stage column to add a deal. When it moves forward, drag the card to the next column. That drag updates the stage, which is what the workflow in section 06 listens for.

05Bring your data

Bring your contacts in.

Import companies first, then people, then deals. Each one links to the one before it, so the order matters.

From a spreadsheet

  • Press Cmd+K on a Mac or Ctrl+K on Windows, type "import" and choose Import records. Or open People and click ⋮ → Import records.
  • Click Download sample file first to see the exact columns Twenty expects. It takes CSV, XLSX or XLS, up to 10,000 records per file.
  • Remove duplicates before you upload. Two people can't share an email, and two companies can't share a domain.
  • Write company domains as https://acme.com, not acme.com. It stops duplicates when your email sync starts creating companies too.
  • To link each person to their company, add a companyDomain column and map it to the Company field.
  • Any custom field has to exist in Settings → Data Model before you import. If your data names an owner for each record, invite your team first.

A People file that links to companies looks like this, from Twenty's guide:

firstName,lastName,email,jobTitle,companyDomain
John,Smith,john@acme.com,CEO,https://acme.com
Jane,Doe,jane@widgets.co,CTO,https://widgets.co

Twenty checks the file before it imports and highlights problem rows in yellow. You can fix a cell right there or skip the row.

From Salesforce

  • 1Export from Salesforce.

    In Salesforce Setup, type "Data Export" in the Quick Find box, choose Data Export, then Export Now. Salesforce emails you when a zip of CSV files is ready, and the zip is deleted 48 hours later, so download it quickly. Data Loader, Salesforce's separate app, is the other way out. Trailhead's Export Data unit walks through both.

  • 2Clean it before you move it.

    Keep active contacts and companies, open deals, the notes that matter and the custom fields you actually use. Leave out contacts with no activity in two years, duplicates and test data. Twenty's migration guide treats the move as a fresh start.

  • 3Import in order.

    Accounts go in as Companies, then Contacts as People, then Opportunities, then notes and tasks. Twenty's comparison page says its importer handles Salesforce exports out of the box. Over 10,000 records, split the file. Over 50,000, the docs recommend the API.

  • 4Rebuild what doesn't come across.

    Views, workflows and permissions don't transfer, and attachments aren't in CSV exports. Keep Salesforce running until you've compared record counts and checked a few people and companies by hand.

06Workflows and AI

Your first workflow, then the AI.

Start with one automation for a job you do every week. A good first one: when a deal is won, create the onboarding task for you.

  • 1Create the workflow.

    Open Workflows in the sidebar, below your other objects, click + New Record and call it "Deal won".

  • 2Set the trigger.

    Choose Record is Updated, pick Opportunities, and under fields to monitor choose Stage. Now it runs every time a deal changes stage.

  • 3Only carry on for won deals.

    Add a Filter step where the new stage equals your won stage. In Twenty's own example the condition is {{trigger.properties.after.stage}} equals "Closed Won".

  • 4Create the task.

    Add a Create Record step on Tasks. Title it "Onboarding:" followed by the deal name from the variable picker, set it due three days out and link it to the company and the deal.

  • 5Test it, then switch it on.

    Click Test, move a test deal to your won stage and check the task appears. Then toggle the workflow to Active.

From there, Twenty's docs have recipes for a fuller deal-won flow and a daily stale-deal alert. Workflows use credits when they run, not when you build them. On Twenty's cloud, monthly billing includes 5 credits a month and yearly billing 50 a year. Simple steps use very little; AI and code steps use more.

The AI

  • 1Ask your CRM questions.

    The AI chat answers questions about your data in plain English, and it knows which record you're looking at. Examples from Twenty's docs:

    Which deals have been in Negotiation for more than 30 days?
    Summarize my interactions with this person
  • 2Put an AI agent in a workflow.

    Add an AI Agent step to any workflow and write the prompt it follows. Twenty's examples: sort inbound leads into categories, enrich company records from public sources, draft follow-up emails from meeting notes and score deals.

  • 3Decide what the AI can see.

    AI agents work inside the same permission system as your team. Give each one a role in Settings → Members → Roles to set what it can read and change.

  • 4Choose the model.

    On the cloud, Twenty runs the models on its own accounts and you pick which ones under Settings → AI. Self-hosted, you bring your own key, and it works on the free plan: add OPENAI_API_KEY, ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, GOOGLE_API_KEY, MISTRAL_API_KEY or XAI_API_KEY in Settings → Admin Panel → Configuration Variables, or in .env followed by a restart. An API key is the password that lets Twenty use that provider's AI, and the provider bills you for it.

Twenty's pricing table also lists an MCP server on every plan. MCP is the standard way AI tools such as Claude plug into other apps.
07Tips

Habits that make a CRM pay for itself.

A CRM only earns its keep if what's in it is current. None of these take more than a few minutes a day.

  • 1Let the mailbox do the typing.

    With email and calendar connected, every conversation with a customer lands on their record. Nobody has to log a call to keep the history.

  • 2Give every open deal a close date you believe.

    Twenty's own pipeline advice: keep close dates realistic, update deals as they move, and close stale ones promptly. A pipeline full of dead deals tells you nothing.

  • 3Write down what each stage means.

    "Proposal" should mean the same thing to everyone. Clear rules for each stage are what make the column totals worth reading.

  • 4Build on People, Companies and Opportunities.

    Those three show synced emails and meetings. Add a field such as Person Type to sort people, rather than a new object that won't carry the email history.

  • 5Make two views you'll actually open.

    "My Pipeline" filtered to Owner is Me, and "Closing This Month" as a table sorted by close date. Then pick a fixed time each week to go through them.

  • 6Automate the nag.

    Once the deal-won workflow works, add the stale-deal alert. Then the CRM reminds you about cold deals and you don't have to remember.

09What to do now

Pick your starting point.

Reading this won't change your CRM. Start with the line that fits where you are.

  • If you want to try it today: start a cloud trial at app.twenty.com, connect your mailbox and build the pipeline (section 04).
  • If you're paying for Salesforce: export your data, import a test batch into a trial and run both side by side for a couple of weeks (section 05).
  • If you want it free: try the one-line Docker install on your laptop, then move it to a server with HTTPS and daily backups (section 03).
  • If you already use Twenty: build the deal-won workflow, then ask the AI chat about your stuck deals (section 06).
